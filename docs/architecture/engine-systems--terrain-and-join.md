# Kinetic Architecture: Engine Systems (Distant Terrain to Modules)

Part of the [Kinetic architecture](../ARCHITECTURE.md).

### Distant Terrain
Phase 6 keeps six quadtree levels of 64-square tiles. Eight-byte samples hold signed height, RGB24, two four-bit light values, water depth and source/coverage flags. Known void is complete coverage without geometry. Priority is client > saved > seeded > approximate; incomplete footprints cannot replace complete ones. Optional `LodLayers` retain at most eight descending intervals per column at levels 0 to 2, with a signed bottom and separate side RGB. Higher levels retain the cheaper single surface. The single minimum-priority `TerrainWorker` exclusively owns mutable tiles; immutable layer arrays and owned copied samples cross worker boundaries. Client capture checks 1 ms between columns, admits 32 copied chunks, rediscovers loaded chunks after resets and recaptures block/light dirtiness. Model reload invalidates palettes and all work epochs; shutdown stops generation and closes GPU resources before the device.

`GeneratedColumns` computes seed tiles from `ChunkGenerator.getBaseHeight` on a sparse grid plus biome colours, coarsest level first. It never reads saved chunks or runs world generation stages: reading saved chunks through the live `ChunkMap` storage and private feature generation were removed on 2026-10-21 after they froze the integrated server for up to 24 s in the overlook benchmark scene. Seen client chunks still replace generated samples.


`TerrainCache` initializes and fingerprints only on workers. Cache identity includes world path, seed, dimension, serialized generator, structure option, data version, quality, separately delimited mod identifiers/versions, data-pack JSON/structure NBT, the frozen resolved material palette and client block textures/models/blockstates/biome colour maps. `LodDiskCache` separates preview/exact entries, writes version/key/CRC headers and level-1 DEFLATE payloads, bounds inflation and interval counts, removes corrupt entries and publishes unique temporary files atomically with concurrent-writer tolerance. Each identity trims toward 512 MiB at most once per minute when writing. Only deterministic seed/preview data is persisted; current saved/client edits are read separately. Complete saved patches avoid generation. Resource reload changes the identity and cancels old jobs.

`LodMesher` merges matching top rectangles and wall runs. Layered meshing subtracts adjacent occupied intervals, retains undersides/air gaps and underwater ground, and uses actual side material colours. A layered result above 65536 quads falls back to its heightfield to cap a mesh at 4 MiB. `LodBorders` retains cross-level adaptive skirts and border invalidation. A uniform heightfield remains five quads/320 bytes. Vertices remain 16 bytes: float XYZ and RGB plus a metadata byte carrying sky4/block3/water1; directional shading is calculated in shaders. Material palettes are built on the client under a separate 1 ms/256-state tick budget from alpha-weighted linear texture samples. Workers access copied integers; unsupported isolated tint callbacks use the base colour. Saved/client light is retained, seed skylight is an exposed/shaded estimate with correct no-sky dimension handling, and emissive surfaces retain block emission.

`LodSelection` waits for complete uploaded children, including zero-geometry coverage. The adapter keeps LOD beneath the view area and uses `LodCoverage` instead of the old unconditional clip square: all captured top-surface sections of a chunk must have uploaded meshes before a 250 ms delay and 250 ms mask fade. A 256-square R8 ring mask updates at most every 25 ms; no world chunk is loaded by the readiness hook. `LodTransitions` partitions old/new regions, fills formerly missing areas and uses complementary screen-stable 250 ms dithering for parent/child and mesh replacements. It retains outgoing buffers until their fade finishes. This is a dissolve, not vertex geomorphing; visual seamlessness still needs in-game validation.

`TerrainGeometry` allocates at most eight 16 MiB vertex pages. `TerrainDraws` streams 36-byte instances and 20-byte indexed indirect commands, batches opaque draws by page and sorts water by tile distance. Staging is capped at 32 MiB and reused only after zero-timeout fence polls; exhaustion defers submission instead of waiting. The ready queue is capped at 24 meshes/32 MiB plus one deferred upload. Uploads check six meshes/2 MiB/1 ms between indivisible driver calls. Once per second the store trims toward 2048 tiles/128 MiB including layers; transient generation, queues, palettes and GPU allocations are separate. GPU bounds include all retained vertical geometry and skirts for conservative frustum rejection. Pressure eviction removes unused, unretained meshes. These are component bounds, not total-memory measurements.

The forward shader uses the current vanilla lightmap and fog. Helion receives normal, sky/block light and unshaded albedo in its four-target pass; distant water is drawn opaque (in Helion after deferred lighting) so tile edges and skirts never show through it, and is also drawn into Helion's water surface pass (`HelionDistantWaterSurfaceMixin`, 2026-10-25), so with Helion's realistic water the composite replaces the flat forward water with the same reflections, glints and depth color as near water. `DistantTerrainFog` stretches the atmospheric environmental fog (1024 blocks by default) and the render-distance fog to the selected distance. `SectionOcclusionGraphDistantTerrainMixin` lifts vanilla's vertical render-distance limit while distant terrain draws: vanilla stops the section graph 12 sections above or below the camera at render distance 12, which left a hole under a high camera where the coverage mask had already hidden distant terrain.

The 2026-10-20 video regression fixes separate sample residency from uploaded geometry lifetime: CPU trimming cannot invalidate an uploaded selection or force its complete children back to a coarse parent, and pressure eviction skips meshes used in the immediately preceding frame before the current frame's selection runs. Complete incoming footprints repair incomplete higher-authority ancestors; among equally complete footprints client > saved > seed > preview still applies. Missing preview work runs first, exact refinement next, and saved refresh uses remaining capacity instead of starving refinement. Layered column capture skips empty air sections and clamps continuous solid ground spans to the level floor so underground caves do not prevent surface greedy merging or overflow the layered quad budget. Cache identity revision 4 invalidates earlier cached tiles. Real-mesh requirements are queued independently on chunk load/discovery and section upload, then obtained from loaded heightmaps at most 64 columns/1 ms per frame; initial mesh lookup also verifies its section coordinates. Full layered capture is no longer needed before masking ready real terrain.

Historical 2026-10-18 benchmark values predate these changes and do not validate them. The historical review in `lod-review--research.md` and `lod-review--verification.md` records the research, tests, supplied regression video and remaining visual/performance work. GPU occlusion, geometric morphing, a dedicated-server LOD channel and dynamic resolution remain separate work; no claim of visual parity or lower total CPU/RAM/GPU time than the references is established by compilation and engine tests.

### Compact Terrain Vertices
Vanilla stores every terrain vertex in 28 bytes (three floats for the position, color, two floats for the texture, two shorts for light). The module `compactVertices` (on by default since the measurements of 2026-10-15 below; off by itself when the installed Helion cannot read the format) stores solid and cutout terrain in 16 bytes, 43 % less vertex memory and upload:

| Attribute | Format | Content |
|---|---|---|
| `PositionLight` | RGBA16_UINT | x, y, z in 1/2048 block steps over -8 to +24 blocks of the section; block light in the low and sky light in the high byte of w |
| `Color` | RGBA8_UNORM | unchanged |
| `PackedUV0` | RG16_UINT | atlas coordinates times 65536 |

1. The pipelines decide the format: `ChunkSectionLayer` takes its vertex size from the vanilla solid and cutout pipelines, so the module gives exactly those four pipelines (direct and multi-draw) the compact format, the define `KINETIC_COMPACT_VERTICES=1` and the vertex shader `kinetic:terrain/compact` while `RenderPipelines` is set up. The chunk buffers and draw offsets follow on their own.
2. Chunks are still built in the vanilla format, so face sorting and NeoForge geometry renderers see vanilla data; right before the upload `CompactVertexEncoder` rewrites the mesh in place.
3. Translucent terrain keeps the vanilla format, because vanilla sorts translucent quads from their float positions.
4. Helion reads the define, not Kinetic code: its geometry pipelines inherit the format with `toBuilder()`, its shadow pipelines copy it from the vanilla pipeline, and its terrain shaders decode under `#ifdef KINETIC_COMPACT_VERTICES`. Helion turns its geometry buffer and shadows off for a format version it does not know.
5. Exactness: model coordinates on the 1/16 grid and texture coordinates of a power-of-two atlas are exact; other positions move by at most 1/4096 block, other texture coordinates round toward the inside of their quad, so no face samples the neighbouring sprite. Vanilla models stay within one block of their block, far inside the range; a vertex outside it is moved to the edge and logged once.

Measured on Can's PC with Helion and Arkea, 1920x1009 window, render distance 32 (2026-10-15, overall average / 1% low / 0.1% low): four runs with the module off 106.1 / 48.1 / 29.7, 104.3 / 46.1 / 27.1, 104.6 / 44.4 / 27.8 and 104.8 / 45.5 / 26.4; two runs on 110.6 / 47.8 / 31.2 and 109.8 / 46.6 / 27.9; peak video memory underground 2375 MB off, 1607 MB on. Per scene the lows of the on runs stay inside the spread of the off runs (elytra 1% low 47.6 to 50.9 on, 48.3 to 55.2 off; underground 35.5 to 37.1 on, 30.9 to 33.2 off). With the two pairs of 2026-10-14 (fullscreen) the overall lows went up in three of four pairs, so the module is on by default.

Older Helion builds cannot read the format. `MixinModules` therefore keeps the module off when Helion is installed without `com/aryston/helion/integration/vanilla/CompactTerrainVertices` in its jar, and logs why.

### Parallel Feature Generation
Vanilla generates noise, surface and biomes of new chunks on the shared background pool, but runs every other step, including features (about a fifth of the generation CPU), one task after another on its `worldgen` executor; with 16 threads that single thread decides how fast new chunks appear. The module `parallelFeatures` moves the feature step to its own job system. A feature step may write only into the 3 x 3 chunks around its own (`blockStateWriteRadius(1)`), so each step holds those nine chunks in `RegionLocks` while it runs, and steps whose areas overlap wait for each other. The other steps keep vanilla's order: structure starts and references and biomes only write the chunk they run for, and that chunk is never inside the area of a running feature step, because a feature step needs its neighbours at the terrain status, which comes after them. Measured with the worldgen check on Can's PC (dedicated server, 2026-10-15): 625 chunks 6115 and 6414 ms off, 4752 and 4924 ms on; 2401 chunks 15258 ms off, 11045 and 11190 ms on; terrain hash identical, no errors. In singleplayer it cost frames: the integrated server shares the processor with the render thread and the chunk builders, and two windowed benchmark pairs (1920x1009, 2026-10-15) gave elytra 108.5 and 101.5 FPS (1% low 41.2 and 38.0) on against 124.1 and 124.2 (52.2 and 55.2) off, overall 1% low 42.6 and 43.1 against 48.1 and 46.1. The mixin is therefore listed under `server` in the mixin configuration and only applies on dedicated servers.

### Packet Compression
The module `networkCompression` gives every connection's `CompressionEncoder` a zlib level 4 deflater instead of level 6. On 1024 real chunks (45 MB of chunk data from the benchmark world) level 6 took 834 ms and gave 14.0 %, level 4 399 ms and 14.6 %, level 1 251 ms and 16.8 % (Can's PC, 2026-10-15). Deflating straight from the packet buffer instead of vanilla's copy into a byte array gave 1 to 2 %, within noise, so it was not kept. Vanilla already holds back flushes during the server tick (`suspendFlushing`) and flushes once per tick, so flush batching has nothing left to gain. Singleplayer connections are in memory and never compress.

### Pregeneration on Join
Vanilla prepares only the chunks within 3 of the spawn while the player is still in the configuration phase (`PrepareSpawnTask`, a `PLAYER_SPAWN` ticket) and closes the loading screen once the chunk under the player is there; the rest of the render distance is generated, sent, lit and built while the player already plays. The module `spawnPregeneration` moves that work in front of the entry, for the owner of an integrated server only:

1. Radius: `SpawnPregenerationSettings` holds the config (on or off, 6 to 32, off during the benchmark) and the client's render distance, and `joinRadius()` is the smaller of the two. The client writes its render distance when the loading screen adopts the tracker, because an integrated server that has no player yet is paused and still reports view distance 10 until its first unpaused tick; both sides run in the same process.
2. Server (`PrepareSpawnTaskPreparingMixin`, `PrepareSpawnTaskPregenerationMixin`, `SpawnPregeneration`): the spawn ticket gets radius `joinRadius() + 1`, because the server sends a chunk only at ticking level, which a ticket of radius r gives to radius r - 1 (without the extra ring the last ring never arrived). The loading listener counts these chunks, the task does not spawn the player before they are loaded, `keepAlive` refreshes the ticket every tick and the ticket expires like vanilla's after the spawn. `ServerGamePacketListenerTerrainWaitMixin` pauses the 60 tick client load timeout for the owner while the client waits.
3. Client (`LevelLoadTrackerPregenerationMixin`, `JoinPregeneration`, `TerrainReadiness`, `PregenerationHold`): when the client load starts, `isLevelReady` stays false until every chunk within the radius is on the client, every non-empty section within radius - 1 that the occlusion graph reaches has a mesh, the client light queue is empty, and this held for the chunk fade-in time. `TerrainReadiness` checks a few cells of its grid per frame (1.5 ms) and keeps running totals, so a check never walks the whole radius. A hold without progress for 15 s or longer than 90 s lets the player in and logs a warning. The progress bar shows 60 % server progress and 40 % terrain readiness (`JoinProgress`).
4. While the client holds (`JoinPregeneration.spreadsWork`), three vanilla bursts are spread over frames. `PacketProcessorPregenerationMixin` handles queued packets for at most 10 ms per frame: a memory connection delivers every chunk at once and vanilla handled them in one frame (815 ms at render distance 32). `ClientLevelPregenerationMixin` runs light updates for at most 6 ms; vanilla runs the whole queue once it holds 1000 entries. `LevelExtractorPregenerationMixin` replaces the sections handed to compiling: the occlusion graph only grows through compiled sections, so with the visible list the terrain arrived in waves; instead up to 64 dirty sections per frame come from a copy of every graph node (refreshed every 100 ms), uncompiled sections whose neighbours are loaded first.

Measured on Can's PC with Arkea and Helion (2026-10-17, existing world, render distance 12, windowed): vanilla entered after 4.3 to 5.2 s with 365 to 432 chunks missing and 29 to 52 visible sections not built, and the first 10 s in game ran at 232 to 248 FPS with a 1% low of 35 to 41; with the module 8.2 to 9.1 s, 0 missing, 0 not built, 373 to 389 FPS and a 1% low of 139 to 165. Render distance 32: entry after 18.2 to 21.2 s with 0 chunks missing. The join itself still has 11 to 15 frames over 50 ms, the longest 200 to 340 ms in the server start and the first terrain frames (level creation, first uploads).

### Join Warm-up
Two lazy steps of the first join run ahead of time once the game has loaded. `JoinWarmup` (module `joinWarmup`) loads every class listed in `assets/kinetic/warmup/join_classes.txt` with `Class.forName(name, false, loader)` on a minimum priority daemon thread and creates one Netty `DefaultChannelId`, whose first use reads the network interfaces. The list holds the 1721 classes the render thread loaded during a join on Can's PC; class loading inside long join frames fell from 70 to 156 ms to 4 to 50 ms and the frame that opens the progress screen from 111 to 185 ms to 65 ms, while the warm-up itself took 467 ms in the background. `GpuCulling.warmUp` compiles the culling compute pipeline on the job system as soon as the device exists; until it is ready the CPU draw list draws the terrain, so the first world frame no longer compiles it.

### Modules
Optimizations that change game classes are mixins grouped into modules (`OptimizationModule`). `MixinModules` reads `config/kinetic-modules.properties` before any mixin applies and writes every switch back; renderer modules also stay off with Sodium or Iris. Features that run later (dynamic frame rate) use the normal client config.

`preciseFrameTime` uses nanosecond timing for the client timer. Vanilla feeds the timer millisecond time, which makes partial ticks advance in 1 ms steps at high frame rates even when frame times look even. After client ticks and swapchain image acquisition, it advances the partial tick to the time the frame will show when that remains inside the current tick.

`particleCollisions` is a non-renderer module. `ParticleCollisionMixin` wraps the block collision call in `Particle.move`; when the same swept, widened and one-block-expanded cuboid vanilla would inspect has at most 256 loaded air cells, it returns the unchanged movement, exactly as vanilla does for an empty collider list. Otherwise vanilla runs. In the 2026-10-18 JFR stress-particles scene, particle movement was 40 % of slow frames, almost all in building the block collider list for `CampfireSmokeParticle.tick`.

`entityCollisions` now applies on both sides. `EntityCollisionShapes` shares the server and client logic, using each level's entity section storage to skip the entity search only when no nearby section can hold a collidable entity, with the same audit. In the explosion and server scenes, client `ItemEntity` and `PrimedTnt` ticks spent most of their time in that search.

`modelCorners` is a renderer module. `ModelCubeCornersMixin` replaces `ModelPart.Cube.compile`, where no API exists, with `CubeCorners`: each distinct cube corner is transformed once and then written in the same vertex order with the same normals. `SharedCorners` identifies positions by exact float bits. Cubes with more than 64 distinct positions use the per-vertex path. In the stress crowd and storage scenes, `Vector3f.mulPosition` below the vanilla compile path used about 10 % of render-thread time for entity and block entity models.

`teamlessPushing` applies on both sides. `EntitySelectorPushMixin` bypasses team lookups only when the level scoreboard has no teams. `PushRules` then keeps the vanilla predicate order: not spectator, pushable, and on the client only the local player. In dense crowds, this predicate was the hottest server-thread frame.
